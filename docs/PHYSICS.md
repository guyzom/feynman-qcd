# Physics scope

The animation introduces selected ideas from quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the quantum field theory of the strong interaction. It uses simplified diagrams and controlled animation to connect color charge, gluon interactions, and coherent addition of amplitudes.

## Color and gluons

Red, green, and blue label the three basis states of quark color; they do not describe visible colors. The diagrams use color–anticolor labels to illustrate changes in a quark's color during gluon emission and absorption.

This notation is schematic. The gluon field has eight independent components associated with the generators of SU(3); it is not a set of nine independent color–anticolor particles. The displayed paths are selected illustrations rather than an enumeration of all allowed diagrams.

The quark–gluon vertex is shown with its coupling, Dirac matrix, and color generator. The gluon self-coupling section highlights the non-Abelian structure of QCD, whose field-strength tensor contains a term proportional to the structure constants. The displayed Lagrangian is a compact form with a quark field; a multi-flavor formulation includes the corresponding flavor sum. Gauge-fixing and ghost terms are outside the presentation.

## Amplitudes and interference

Contributions to a quantum transition combine at the amplitude level. Probabilities or rates depend on the squared magnitude of the combined amplitude, together with the appropriate normalization and kinematic factors. Adding squared magnitudes separately would omit the interference terms.

The animation displays this distinction through a symbolic sum and a head-to-tail chain of complex phasors. Their phases and lengths are prescribed for the visual explanation. They are not evaluated from a QCD action or a scattering calculation. The spiral and stationary-phase labels illustrate cancellation and coherent addition; they do not establish a physical trajectory or compute a path integral.

The time-slice section presents color superposition as an explanatory picture. It does not simulate a gauge-invariant state or model confinement, hadron structure, running couplings, or nonperturbative dynamics.
